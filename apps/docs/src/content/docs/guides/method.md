Atomic is built around a few ideas from James Clear's *Atomic Habits*. This page explains how
each one shows up in the app. A short **principle of the day** from the book sits at the top of
Today, and the most relevant principle opens the Identity screen, the Journal and each habit's
blueprint.

## Identity-based habits

> Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become.

Identities ("I am a person who never misses a workout") sit at the top of your system, so the
habit editor starts there: *Who do you want to become?* comes before *What will you do?*. Each habit
casts votes for one identity:

- **Build habits** earn one vote per repetition, up to the daily goal.
- **Break habits** earn one vote per clean day.

A habit wears its identity's icon (🌱 for a new build habit, 🚫 for a break habit, until you pick an
identity or an icon of your own). The **Identity** screen shows each identity's votes and recent
momentum, the evidence you wrote down in the [journal](/guides/journal/), and your values from
your last Integrity Report.

## The four laws of behavior change

The habit editor walks through the four laws, and their inversions when you want to break a habit.

| Law | Build a habit | Break a habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st — Cue | **Make it obvious**: implementation intention (time + place), habit stacking ("After I pour my coffee…"), environment design | **Make it invisible**: name the trigger, reduce your exposure to it |
| 2nd — Craving | **Make it attractive**: temptation bundling, "I get to…" reframing | **Make it unattractive**: reframe the benefits of avoiding it |
| 3rd — Response | **Make it easy**: the two-minute version | **Make it difficult**: increase friction |
| 4th — Reward | **Make it satisfying**: an immediate reward | **Make it unsatisfying**: a habit contract |

### The blueprint board

Every habit's page shows its **blueprint** as a board with one column per law ("Make it obvious ·
Cue"). Each slot shows what you wrote, with the book's concept underneath; an empty slot shows the
concept, ready to fill. Click or tap a slot to edit it in place; **Fill next** opens the next empty
one. The board's header quotes the principle of your weakest law.

| Law | Build slots | Break slots |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Implementation intention · when, Implementation intention · where, Habit stacking, Environment design | Pointing-and-calling, Reduce exposure |
| 2 | Temptation bundling, Reframe: "I get to" | Reframe the benefits |
| 3 | Two-minute rule | Increase friction |
| 4 | Immediate reward | Habit contract |

The readable version still exists, for example:

> I will read 10 pages at 21:30 in bed. After I brush my teeth, I will read 10 pages.
> Two-minute version: read one page.

### Setup tasks: prepare the environment

Designing your environment is often a one-time job: put the book on your pillow, uninstall the app,
leave the running shoes by the door. While you create a habit, the environment field (and, for a
break habit, the friction and contract fields) can also become a **task**, one-off or repeating,
and you can add free-form setup tasks to any law. They show up in your Tasks and on the habit's
page under **Setup tasks**; the board offers **Make it a task** for a slot you've filled. If you
later change the text, Atomic offers to update the task too. Setup tasks never count as votes.

## Never miss twice

Missing once is an accident; missing twice is the start of a new habit. Atomic's streaks follow
that rule:

- A single missed scheduled day **does not reset** your streak (it just doesn't add to it), and
  Today shows a *Never miss twice* card, with **Plan the comeback** (a short journal entry: when,
  where, after what, and the two-minute version). Your plan then appears on that card.
- Two misses in a row end the streak; the card becomes a fresh start.
- **Skipped** days (an intentional rest day) are neutral.
- Weekly habits ("3 times a week") count streaks in weeks.
- **Break habits** count clean days instead: a slip restarts the count, and Atomic invites you to
  reflect on what triggered it.

## The monthly goal

A daily goal is fragile: one short day and it's gone. Each habit also has a **monthly goal**, the
sum of what its schedule asks for this month, and every day counts toward it:

- **Partial days count** what you did: 6 of 10 pages is 6 pages toward the month.
- **Extra reps count**, up to twice the daily goal on a day, so a strong day makes up for a short
  one. Reps on unscheduled days count too. Skipped days owe nothing.
- The month card on the habit's page says where you stand: ahead, on track, or behind with the pace
  you need ("12 to go · 3/day over the 4 days left"). When the month can no longer be won, it says
  so calmly: *never miss twice*, next month starts fresh.
- Reaching the goal is celebrated once. **Months won** show on the habit's page and in Insights.
  Break habits get a count of clean days instead of a goal.
- Changing a habit's goal never rewrites past months: each month keeps the goal it had.

## The Goldilocks rule: level up

Motivation peaks when the challenge sits at the edge of your ability. After a month where you
beat your daily goal on most days, Atomic suggests **raising it**, and shows the evidence ("You
beat 10 pages on 24 of 30 days in September"). For a weekly habit it suggests one more session a
week. You can accept, say "Not now" (after two "Not now"s in a row it stops asking for a few
months), or do a **Goldilocks check-in** in the journal to decide whether the habit is too easy,
too hard or just right.

## Habit strength and freedom

Streaks are fragile; habits are about repetitions. **Habit strength** is an exponential moving
average of your recent outcomes with a half-life of 14 scheduled days (3 weeks for weekly habits).
Around 66 consistent days gets you to ~96%, a nod to research on how long habits take to become
automatic. For break habits the same measure is called **Freedom**: how free you're becoming,
based on recent clean days.

## 1% better every day

Insights compares three curves over the days you've been building your system: 1% better every day
(1.01ⁿ), **staying the same** (a flat 1.0× line, because standing still is a choice too) and 1%
worse (0.99ⁿ), next to your real consistency, perfect days, votes and months won.

Right below it, **the plateau of latent potential** explains why results lag behind effort: like
an ice cube that doesn't melt until 32 °F, the work you put in accumulates before it shows.

**Strongest habits** lists only the habits you're building; the ones you're breaking get their own
list, **Breaking free**, ranked by their current clean run.

## Habits done inside the app

Some habits are best done right in Atomic: log an expense, time a meditation, write 300 words,
note a slip and its cue. **Tracking is part of making it satisfying**: the entries become the day's
progress, with weekly and monthly summaries. See [Trackers](/guides/trackers/).

## The journal

The book is full of small writing exercises: proof of an identity, a comeback plan, a habit
contract, the Annual Review and the Integrity Report. The [journal](/guides/journal/) turns each one
into a guided entry tied to your identities and habits.

## Habits Scorecard

Before changing habits you need to notice them. The **Scorecard** lists what you do on a normal
day, each rated positive, neutral or negative, and turns any line into a habit to build or break.
The journal's **Notice a habit** adds lines to it as you spot them.
