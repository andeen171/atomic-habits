Atomic is built around a few ideas from James Clear's *Atomic Habits*. This page explains how
each one shows up in the app.

## Identity-based habits

> Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become.

Identities ("I am a person who never misses a workout") sit at the top of your system. Each habit
can cast votes for one identity:

- **Build habits** earn one vote per repetition, up to the daily goal.
- **Break habits** earn one vote per clean day.

The **Identity** screen shows the total votes and recent momentum for each identity.

## The four laws of behavior change

The habit editor follows the four laws — and their inversions when you want to break a habit.

| Law | Build a habit | Break a habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st — Cue | **Make it obvious**: implementation intention (time + location), habit stacking ("After I pour my coffee…"), environment design | **Make it invisible**: name the trigger, remove it from your environment |
| 2nd — Craving | **Make it attractive**: temptation bundling, "I get to…" reframing | **Make it unattractive**: spell out the real cost |
| 3rd — Response | **Make it easy**: the two-minute version | **Make it difficult**: add friction |
| 4th — Reward | **Make it satisfying**: an immediate reward, tracking | **Make it unsatisfying**: accountability |

Every habit gets a readable **Blueprint** built from your answers, for example:

> I will read 10 pages at 21:30 in bed. After I brush my teeth, I will read 10 pages.
> Two-minute version: read one page.

## Never miss twice

Missing once is an accident; missing twice is the start of a new habit. Atomic's streaks follow
that rule:

- A single missed scheduled day **does not reset** your streak (it just doesn't add to it), and
  Today shows a *Never miss twice* nudge.
- Two misses in a row end the streak.
- **Skipped** days (an intentional rest day) are neutral.
- Weekly habits ("3 times a week") count streaks in weeks.

## Habit strength

Streaks are fragile; habits are about repetitions. **Habit strength** is an exponential moving
average of your recent outcomes with a half-life of 14 scheduled days (3 weeks for weekly
habits). Around 66 consistent days gets you to ~96% — a nod to research on how long habits take
to become automatic.

## 1% better every day

Insights compares 1.01ⁿ with 0.99ⁿ over the days you've been building your system — the
compound interest of self-improvement — next to your real consistency, perfect days and votes.

## Habits Scorecard

Before changing habits you need to notice them. The **Scorecard** lists what you do on a normal
day, each rated positive, neutral or negative, and turns any line into a habit to build or break.
